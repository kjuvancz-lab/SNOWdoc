In today's dynamic digital landscape, new technologies emerge rapidly, promising increased productivity and
operational efficiency. However, the mere deployment of these tools is not enough to guarantee transformative
results. Effective Organizational Change Management (OCM) is crucial to ensure that employees, those most
impacted by technological shifts, are ready and motivated to embrace new ways of working. This section explores
why OCM is indispensable, how to implement it strategically using Kotter's 8-Phase Change Model22, and the
outcomes you can expect from a well-planned approach to change.

12.1 Why Organizational Change Management is critical
OCM fundamentally prepares people for change. No matter how advanced or promising a technology might be, its
success hinges on user adoption. Without a proactive OCM plan, organizations risk:

     · Underutilization of Technology: Missing out on the full suite of features and benefits.
     · Delayed Project Timelines: Encounters with resistance or reluctance to adopt new processes.
     · Subpar User Adoption Rates: Employees revert to familiar tools and workflows, negating the intended

          gains.

12.2 The strategic importance of OCM
OCM extends far beyond mere adoption; it builds the organizational capacity to handle change sustainably and
iteratively. In a rapidly shifting business environment, agility is paramount. According to ServiceNow's insights:

      Organizations that excel in OCM adopt new technologies more effectively.
      A strong OCM framework fosters a culture of resilience and continuous improvement.
      Effective OCM breaks down silos, aligning people, processes, and technologies toward common goals.

For this part we want to reference John P. Kotter's 8-phase Change Model23.

     1. Create Urgency
                o Action: Develop a compelling narrative about why change is imperative, backed by real-world data
                     and case studies.
                o Benefit: Sparks the initial motivation and highlights what's at stake.

     2. Form a Powerful Coalition

                o Benefit: Provides credibility and momentum for the transformation effort.

     3. Create a Vision for Change
                o Action: Formulate a succinct yet inspiring vision that outlines the future state.
                o Benefit: Aligns stakeholder expectations and makes the directive more tangible.

     4. Communicate the Vision
                o Action: Use every channel - emails, meetings, intranets, and casual encounters - to reinforce the
                     vision.

13 ServiceNow or Never: Decoding the True Worth

"What gets measured gets managed."25

                                                                                      (Drucker, The Practice of Management 1954)

In the fast-paced realm of ServiceNow implementations, success hinges on more than just functional adoption. True
success is measured by how effectively technology investments translate into tangible business outcomes. This is
where Value Management becomes indispensable. By anchoring each ServiceNow initiative to clear, quantifiable
results, organizations can both validate their investments and chart a path toward continuous improvement.

13.1 Why Value Management is crucial

Value Management shifts the perception of ServiceNow from a cost center to a value driver, emphasizing
measurable returns on investment. This approach:

      Aligns Technology with Strategy: Ensures every enhancement supports organizational goals and
          strengthens competitive positioning.

      Secures Ongoing Executive Support: Demonstrates clear financial gains, making budget approvals
          smoother.

      Bridges Technology and Business Performance: Ties operational improvements directly to profitability and
          growth metrics.

13.2 How to Implement Effective Value Management

     1. Define the Value Blueprint
               o Action: Create a comprehensive plan outlining the capabilities to be enhanced - Employee, IT,
                    and Customer Workflows - and articulate how each aligns with business objectives.
               o Benefit: Serves as a single source of truth, guiding both technical execution and broader
                    organizational strategy.

Tip: Couple your Value Blueprint with a clear implementation roadmap, ensuring each capability has defined
milestones, timelines, and performance targets.

     2. Capture and Analyse Baseline Data
                o Action: Gather existing metrics such as resolution times, user satisfaction scores, and operational
                     costs per service ticket.
                o Benefit: Establishes a clear "before" snapshot, enabling accurate measurement of "after"
                     improvements.

     3. Regular Monitoring and Adjustment
                o Action: Schedule quarterly or monthly reviews of ServiceNow's performance data, comparing
                     actual metrics against baseline.
                o Benefit: Maintains alignment with evolving business goals, fostering a culture of continuous
                     optimization.

     4. Translate Improvements into Financial Terms
                o Action: Convert metrics like reduced handling times or lower error rates into direct cost savings or
                     revenue boosts.

14 From Platform to Intelligence: Building an AI-Ready CoEI

14.1 Why AI Readiness matters in the AI CoEI Journey
Artificial Intelligence has become a native and foundational capability of the ServiceNow Platform. What began as
workflow digitization has evolved into intelligent enterprise operations - augmenting people, automating decisions,
and improving how work moves across the company. For organizations investing in a Center of Excellence &
Innovation (CoEI), AI is no longer a "future topic." It is a strategic inflection point.
This chapter builds directly on the AI CoEI foundations already outlined in this whitepaper:

     · Vision  extending from platform-centric transformation to intelligent operations
     · Strategy  positioning AI as a value-creation engine
     · Roadmap  sequencing AI use cases and pre-requisites
     · Governance  adding AI risk, ethics, validation, and lifecycle controls
     · Operating Model  enabling AI delivery, monitoring, and continuous improvement
     · Roles & Skills  expanding with AI product ownership, prompt engineering, and RAG librarianship

AI readiness is the organizational capability to safely deploy, adopt, and scale AI across the enterprise, leveraging
ServiceNow's native AI capabilities (GenAI, AI Search & Retriever, RAG, Agentic AI, AI Control Tower, predictive
analytics, and automation).
It is therefore not about tools - it's about preparation, clarity, people, process, and data.
